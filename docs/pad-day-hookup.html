Hook up & test

  1. Bench check the SH-U10 first

    Meter in ohms across A+ ↔ B−. A reading near 120 Ω means an onboard terminator — since this taps an existing bus mid-span, consider lifting that resistor before install; at minimum, remember it if comms errors show up later.

  2. Breaker off, land the wires — but not the buck's input yet

    Per the diagram and table: data pair + ground to the SH-U10 (one CAT5e twisted pair for yellow/green), red through the fuse, black run in place. Leave the buck's ＋/− input leads unconnected for now, and 5V0 on the SH-U10 stays empty.

  3. Breaker on — qualify the rail before the buck ever sees it

    DC volts, COM red ↔ black: expect roughly 15 V to low-20s. Abort if over 30 V (the buck's ceiling is 32).

  4. Breaker off, connect the buck — then verify its output

    Land the buck's ＋/− (double-check they aren't swapped), breaker back on, and confirm 5.0–5.2 V at its USB-C output before the Pi touches it. Not optional — this is what protects the Pi.

  5. Plug in the Pi

    Buck's USB-C → the power port — the same port that powered it on the bench. SH-U10 → OTG adapter → the other USB-C. SD card in; it boots and joins WiFi on its own. Never plug in a wall charger while the buck is connected — one power source at a time.

  6. Open the dashboard — and let njsPC finish waking up

    On your phone: http://192.168.1.221:8080. First boot at the pad takes a while — njsPC recompiles at startup (several minutes on this chip). Wait until the header stops saying njsPC unreachable. Success = it then names your EasyTouch and real temperatures appear.

  7. njsPC up but still "Nixie" after ~3 more minutes? Swap the pair

    Breaker off, swap yellow ↔ green at the SH-U10 (A+ ↔ B−), power back up. Polarity is the only wiring variable — one of the two ways works. Don't swap until njsPC itself is reachable, or you'll be "fixing" wiring that was right all along.
